Samuel Schapiro

I'm an interdisciplinary scientist and entrepreneur studying the science of intelligence and creativity, affiliated with the Knowledge Lab at the University of Chicago and the AI Innovation Institute (AI3) at Stony Brook University, and closely collaborating with the Department of Psychology at Pennsylvania State University. I am also currently a research scientist at a stealth startup.

My work has been featured in scientific venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, COLM, ICLR, and ICCC and bridges cognitive science, computer science, and the philosophy of science to advance our understanding of creativity, our ability to elicit it in intelligent machines, and its applications towards scientific, industrial, and artistic domains. My work has been recognized with various accolades, including Best Short Paper Award at ICCC 2025, an Oral talk at the Scientific Understanding of Foundation Models Workshop at COLM 2026, and an invited chapter in the Cambridge Handbook of Creativity (3rd Ed.).

Research

My research seeks to advance our understanding of creativity in humans and intelligent machines: how we can measure it, how we can elicit it in AI systems, and what it fundamentally is. This work is organized around three interrelated areas:

See Papers Measuring Creativity in Humans and AI Click to explore →

Benchmarks and tests of creativity, especially more complex forms such as transformational and scientific creativity.

See Papers Improving the Creativity of AI Click to explore →

Methods and systems that make models more creative, from steering model weights to scientific idea generation.

See Papers Theories and Foundations of Creativity Click to explore →

Formal accounts of what creativity is and how it manifests in minds and machines.
